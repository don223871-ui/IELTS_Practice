# Sample Answer · The Sulphur Cycle

The diagram illustrates the main stages of the sulphur cycle, showing how sulphur is released into the environment, transformed in the atmosphere, returned to the land and water, and eventually recycled or stored.

Overall, sulphur enters the atmosphere through both natural processes, particularly volcanic activity, and human activities such as burning fossil fuels and metal processing. It subsequently undergoes atmospheric transformation before being deposited on the Earth’s surface, where it is taken up by plants and animals, recycled through decomposition, or transferred to longer-term reservoirs.

Initially, sulphur is released into the atmosphere, mainly in the form of sulphur dioxide. This occurs naturally through volcanic activity and the release of sulphur compounds from rocks and the ocean, while human activities also contribute substantially to atmospheric sulphur. Once airborne, sulphur dioxide reacts with oxygen and water to form sulphate salts and sulphuric acid. These compounds then return to the Earth's surface through wet and dry deposition.

Following deposition, plants absorb sulphate ions and incorporate sulphur into proteins, which are subsequently consumed by animals. When plants and animals die, microorganisms break down the organic material, releasing sulphur compounds back into the soil and water. Some sulphates are also carried into rivers and ultimately the ocean through runoff. Finally, part of the sulphur becomes stored in the Earth's crust and oceanic sediments, completing the longer-term stage of the cycle.
